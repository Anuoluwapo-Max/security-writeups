# Network Media Types

## Context
Notes from Cisco Networking Academy — Networking Basics, Module 6 
(Network Media).

## The Core Idea
Data has to physically travel somehow. There are three ways to encode 
and transmit it across a network:

| Media Type | How Data is Encoded |
|---|---|
| Copper cables | Electrical impulses |
| Fiber-optic cables | Pulses of light |
| Wireless | Modulation of radio frequencies |

## Key Insight: Why Fiber Beats Interference
Fiber-optic cables are essentially immune to electrical interference. 
Since they carry data as light through glass or plastic (not 
electricity through metal), there's no electrical signal for anything 
to interfere with in the first place. This is why fiber is preferred 
in environments with heavy electrical noise or for long-distance, 
high-speed transmission.

## Choosing the Right Media: Four Deciding Factors
1. **Distance** — maximum range the media can carry a signal reliably
2. **Environment** — physical conditions where the media will be installed
3. **Data volume/speed** — how much data needs to move, and how fast
4. **Cost** — installation and material expense

No single "best" media type — just the right trade-off for the specific 
network's needs.

## Common Cable Types
- **Twisted-pair (copper)** — most common, used for standard Ethernet
- **Coaxial cable** — copper-based, commonly used for cable TV and 
  satellite systems
- **Fiber-optic** — glass or plastic strands about the width of a human 
  hair, carries data at very high speeds over long distances
